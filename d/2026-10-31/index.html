01Technology in finance · Europe

ECB supervisor puts generative AI use at about 85 percent of banks

Pedro Machado, a member of the ECB's Supervisory Board, said in a Bloomberg interview the ECB published on 30 October that around 85 percent of banks already use generative AI, from fraud detection and KYC to credit scoring. He warned that frontier AI models shorten the time between a vulnerability being found and exploited, which makes fast patching banks' biggest challenge. The same day, Executive Board member Philip Lane said the AI boom adds about 0.9 percentage points to euro area credit growth, around a quarter of the total.

In July the ECB asked significant banks for action plans on AI-enabled cyber threats by 31 October; Machado said a fuller assessment will follow once they are in.

Why it matters. Security and AI governance leads at significant euro area banks should expect supervisory follow-up and comparison of their plans across banks.

02Financial services · Switzerland

Swiss finance ministry finds a UBS move abroad would bring little capital relief

An internal assessment by the Federal Department of Finance, reported by Reuters on 29 October, found that UBS would gain little by moving to the US or the UK to escape tougher Swiss capital rules. US holding-company rules would tie up about as much capital as the Swiss proposal, and in the UK the Swiss business would carry much heavier risk weights. The ministry called the Federal Council's proposal effective, targeted, proportionate and manageable for UBS.

Reuters puts the possible extra CET1 need at about $18 billion; the Council of States voted in September for 90 percent backing of foreign units instead of 100.

Why it matters. Lawmakers now have the government's own view that relocating would spare UBS little capital, as the National Council takes up the rules.

Watch next. The National Council committee's debate in November, ahead of a possible vote in December.

03Technology in finance · Global

North Korean hackers blamed for a record $6.3 billion theft from Binance

An attacker moved about $6.36 billion out of Binance wallets on ten blockchains on the evening of 29 October, most of it within about 20 minutes, according to TRM Labs, which attributes the theft with high confidence to TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-sponsored group; Chainalysis agrees. Binance first pointed to a compromised engineer's laptop with access to a service used to submit withdrawals, later found further points of breach, and has set aside over $6.34 billion of its own funds to cover losses.

Around $3 billion has been frozen with partners, Binance says. TRM calls it the largest crypto theft on record, about four times the Bybit hack of February 2025.

Why it matters. Banks and Swiss custodians running digital asset desks will be asked how their hot-wallet signing and engineers' devices would stand up to the same playbook.

Watch next. Whether withdrawals resume on schedule this weekend.

Swiss Bankers Association wants banks to issue franc stablecoins under their licences

In a paper presented in Zurich on 29 October, the Swiss Bankers Association said a Swiss franc stablecoin should be issued from Switzerland under FINMA supervision. It wants banks to issue stablecoins under their existing banking licence, rather than through the separate subsidiary the Federal Council's draft revision of the Financial Institutions Act requires, and more flexible reserve rules.

Banks testing a franc stablecoin in a sandbox since September, among them UBS, PostFinance, Raiffeisen and Zürcher Kantonalbank, would be spared a separately licensed subsidiary if parliament agrees.

Dextra, a Zurich legal expenses insurer handling about 40,000 cases a year, now uses start-up iudexnc's software in regular operations to check whether claims are covered, after a pilot that began in October 2025, startupticker.ch reported. ING chief executive Steven van Rijswijk said on the third-quarter call on 29 October that agentic AI in Dutch mortgages has cut the approval time for complex mortgages from seven days to five, and that ING plans to extend it to other markets. Global

China-linked hackers ran an AI agent framework against more than 200 organisations

CrowdStrike says the China-based group it tracks as Vapor Panda used ARTEX, an AI-driven intrusion framework running on DeepSeek and possibly a second commercial model, against more than 200 organisations in at least 14 countries from 17 October, finance entities among them. Planner and worker agents ran several intrusions at once, with a human operator steering at decision points; CrowdStrike confirmed post-exploitation activity at nine organisations.

Bank security teams should plan for intrusions run in parallel at machine pace, which shortens the time to catch misuse of leaked or unrotated credentials.
