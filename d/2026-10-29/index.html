01Rules and supervisors · Switzerland

Federal Council plans binding AI rules, with consultation due in spring 2027

On 28 October the Federal Council took note of progress in applying the Council of Europe AI Convention, which it decided to ratify in February 2025. OFCOM published a report on sector-specific AI measures and a guide with model texts on 10 topics for voluntary codes of conduct. The Federal Office of Justice is drafting binding rules on transparency, data protection, non-discrimination, supervision and risk assessment, with consultation due to start in spring 2027.

Switzerland chose to ratify the convention and adapt sector laws rather than adopt a horizontal law like the EU AI Act. OFCOM's report says sectors tied to data processing and regulated markets face particular challenges.

Why it matters. Banks now have a dated horizon for binding Swiss AI duties alongside FINMA's supervisory expectations, so AI governance built today should map to those five themes.

Watch next. The Federal Office of Justice draft in spring 2027, and whether banking associations take up OFCOM's code of conduct guide.

02Technology in finance · Switzerland

Swiss banks stay loyal to Finnova and Avaloq but rate their AI poorly

The Institute of Financial Services Zug (IFZ) at Lucerne University of Applied Sciences surveyed IT executives at Swiss and Liechtenstein banks; 63 of 123 responded. 65 percent expect to keep their core banking vendor for ten years, up from 32 percent in 2023, and 68 percent innovate mainly in systems around the core. 41 percent rank AI integration among the top three future requirements, yet core systems score about two out of five on it.

Among respondents, Finnova runs 63 percent of cores and Avaloq 22 percent, according to Inside IT. The authors call the market a duopoly in which new vendors have not broken in.

Why it matters. AI in Swiss banking will arrive mostly in the layer around the core, which favours integration, data access and orchestration work over replacement projects.

Watch next. How Finnova and Avaloq answer with AI roadmaps, and whether the 62 percent who find integration demanding push for better interfaces.

03Technology in finance · Global

Canada's big banks block personal AI agents from online banking

RBC, BMO, TD and CIBC say they do not let third-party personal AI agents access online banking or act for clients, according to The Canadian Press. Scotiabank will restrict such agents while it monitors concerns; National Bank is not blocking them and is watching how they develop. RBC chief executive Dave McKay wrote that nothing is more important than the security of client information.

Consumer agents from the AI labs can now log in to websites and complete tasks for users, which puts credentials and transactions in the hands of software the bank does not control.

Why it matters. This is an early sector-wide stance on agents acting for clients. Swiss and European banks face the same choice between blocking agents and offering approved channels.

Watch next. Whether banks follow with sanctioned agent access, such as delegated logins or APIs with limited permissions.
