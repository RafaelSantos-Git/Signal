01Financial servicesSwitzerland

UBS lifts pre-tax profit 27% and raises its cost-saving goal to $13.7bn

UBS reported third-quarter net profit of $2.69bn, up 8%, and pre-tax profit of $3.59bn, up 27%. Wealth management drew $55.1bn of net new assets. Savings from the Credit Suisse integration reached $13.2bn, and UBS now expects about $13.7bn by the end of 2026, above its earlier ambition of about $13.5bn.

Parliament is debating how much capital UBS must hold against its foreign subsidiaries; the proposal sits with a committee and is expected in the National Council in December, according to Reuters.

Why it matters. UBS has bought back $1.8bn of its $3bn programme and links the rest to that debate, so Parliament's decision now sets the pace of its shareholder returns. About 90% of the legacy Credit Suisse applications in scope are fully decommissioned, the bank said.

Watch next. The National Council's debate on the capital rules, expected in December.

02Technology in financeSwitzerland

Temenos raises its 2026 profit guidance after a strong third quarter

The Geneva banking software maker reported third-quarter revenue of $292.8m, up 13%, and non-IFRS EBIT of $109.3m, up 29%. Annual recurring revenue rose 12% in constant currency to $903.6m. Temenos now expects 2026 EBIT growth of about 12% and earnings per share growth of about 10%, up from about 9% and 7%.

Subscription and SaaS revenue rose 23% to $121.8m; one large deal in Africa slipped into the first days of the fourth quarter.

Why it matters. Temenos's results are a read on how much banks are spending to renew core banking, and its shift to subscriptions shapes what many Swiss and European banks pay for the platforms they run on.

Watch next. Whether the delayed African deal lifts fourth-quarter subscription revenue.

03AI and technologyGlobal

Microsoft offers voluntary departure to about 60,000 staff as AI spending grows

About 60,000 employees, roughly a quarter of Microsoft's workforce, can leave with a severance payment, according to an internal memo reported by Business Insider and covered by ICTjournal. Windows, Xbox, Copilot and finance teams are included; some data centre, sales, security and quantum roles are not, Bloomberg reported.

Earlier this year Microsoft offered a similar programme to about 9,000 employees, and nearly a third accepted.

Why it matters. Bloomberg links the programme to AI infrastructure spending that is squeezing budgets and hiring elsewhere in the company, a trade-off that technology budgets at banks face as well.
